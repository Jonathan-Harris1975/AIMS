# AI Edge editorial quality standard (November 2026)

Goal: become the trusted, useful and unmistakably Gen X AI briefing, not another RSS summary feed.
A model rating its own writing 100/100 is not evidence of a 10/10 publication.

## Mandatory publication controls

1. **Fresh coverage.** Compare weekday candidates against source links and titles in *dispatched* R2 newsletter issues from the previous seven days. Do not confuse generated drafts with delivered copy. If the read fails, log the failure and record incomplete evidence rather than taking down the whole delivery operation. Weekend debate/reflection editions may deliberately revisit the week's topics.
2. **Source integrity.** Enrich selected feed entries with original article text when accessible. Clearly treat the RSS teaser as potentially truncated or wrong, and web page content as untrusted data. Never manufacture a fact, quantitative claim, quote or first-hand test. Quantities unsupported by retrieved article evidence are deterministic QA defects. Missing original text is recorded as unverified, not falsely marked checked.
3. **News judgement.** Score for significance, independent/reputable reporting, reader relevance and freshness. Deprioritise advertorials and self-serving vendor primers. The highest-score candidate is not automatically a Big Three story; the composer still makes the editorial choice.
4. **Voice.** Plain British English, confident but evidence-led. Wry Gen X criticism belongs on exaggerated claims, not ordinary people. Praise genuine progress. Never claim Jonathan personally tested a tool or lived an anecdote unless it is in verified editorial evidence.
5. **Practical return.** Each weekday edition needs a specific action in Actually Useful, supported by the source. Avoid generic "worth watching" filler.
6. **Structural polish.** Hide empty optional sections, reject promotional RSS tails, and validate titles, links, duplication and required sections. Full content review still matters; deterministic gates cannot prove truth, originality or reader satisfaction.
7. **Delivery reliability.** Reuse the existing checkpoints, bounded repairs and sender recovery. Article fetches and historical checks must have timeouts and diagnostic reporting. Do not gate delivery on optional artwork or unrelated services. Headroom stays enabled and unrestricted.

## Monthly independent quality benchmark

For a sample of three AI Edge weekday editions plus one weekend edition, compare with contemporary published editions of The Rundown AI, TLDR AI, The Neuron, One Useful Thing and The Batch. Judge the **actual final emails**, not prompts or QA scores.

Score each dimension 0–10, with source links and short examples:
- Newsworthiness and major-story coverage
- Accuracy and source attribution
- Uniqueness against the previous seven days
- Original judgement and independent scepticism
- Distinctive British Gen X voice
- Practical usefulness
- Readability and five-minute scanability
- Headline/subject strength
- Credible balance between vendors, labs, critics and independent research
- Reader response and audience retention evidence

A 10/10 editorial ambition is a target, not a promise. Even 90+ internal QA should not be reported as top-tier without the independent comparison and audience evidence. Always distinguish internal council scores from actual reader outcomes.

## Operational signals

AIMS's monthly newsletter audit emits: published issue count, seven-day repeated-link incidents, number of issues checked against history, completeness of those checks, and mean count of full article evidence captures per issue. Also track daily missed lead stories, editorial corrections, clicks, replies and unsubscribes when trustworthy provider data exists. One.com SMTP does not supply Brevo-style opens/clicks, so do not invent them.

## Regression cases

- 9 and 10 November 2026 led with the same two stories: must be excluded on the second day.
- 10 November included the unedited DataRobot RSS promotional tail: must be repaired or rejected.
- 11 November AlphaProof Code summary disagreed with the original article's quantitative claim: verified article must take priority over an RSS snippet.
- If a day has fewer than three fresh stories, expand the **already retrieved** window to 72 hours before declining to generate. Never quietly reprint yesterday's piece to fill the template.
- Source fetch failure must not be presented as "verified", and the required reader action must not become an invented first-hand test.

A real editorial review is still needed after launch to establish whether the target has been achieved. Do not assign a new 10/10 score solely because the software passed tests.
